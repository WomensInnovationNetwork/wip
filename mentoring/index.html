What we do

AMPLIFY: twelve weeks, two ways in

We run mentoring in cohorts — twelve weeks, kickoff to Graduation — so everybody moves through it together rather than drifting off after two good conversations. The program is called AMPLIFY, and it is built around the two things members ask for most: confidence and network.

Everyone meets every week at the Power Hour — a lesson, questions, and time to connect. On top of that, everyone is in one of two tracks: Circuits, a small group led by one mentor, for women new to the program; or Power Pairs, one-to-one, for women who have mentored or been mentored before.

What we’ve done so far

Five cohorts in

We have been running mentoring since 2024. Five cohorts have finished, every one of them staffed entirely by volunteers who did it around a full-time job.

Each cohort has changed shape based on what the last one told us. That is the part we are proudest of — not that we got it right first time, but that the feedback form actually changes the program.

Four things five cohorts taught us

Tell people what they signed up for

The loudest complaint we ever got was that people did not know what the sessions would cover. Now every group session topic is published a week ahead, and rooms are labelled foundational or advanced.

Small rooms beat big calls

The breakout rooms were consistently the most valued part of group mentoring, and the long plenary sections the least. So the framing got shorter and the breakouts got longer.

Seniority is not the same as being new

Plenty of women arrive with twenty years of experience but no history with this program. Your track now depends on your mentoring experience, not your CV — and a Circuit is a room to be a beginner in, whatever your seniority.

Everyone has to speak, once

The same handful of women were doing most of the talking. Now every Circuit Member presents once — at the Week 9 Showcase or at Graduation. Five minutes counts, and you rehearse it at your Circuit’s demo day first.

The two tracks

Where you start depends on where you’ve been

Not on how senior you are. Plenty of women arrive with twenty years in the industry and start in a Circuit — placement is about your mentoring experience, not your CV.

Circuits

Group mentoring

For women new to Women in Power, or new to mentoring.

  • A small pod — around six Circuit Members — with one Circuit Lead
  • 45–60 minutes every other week, about six sessions
  • Goal-setting, shared activities, and a demo day to finish
  • Present once, at the Showcase or at Graduation
  • Your Circuit-mates are often the relationships that last longest

Completing a Circuit is what lets you apply for Power Pairs next cohort.

Power Pairs

1:1 mentoring

For Circuit graduates, returning members, and women who have been through an established mentoring program.

  • Matched with one mentor on goals, career stage and interests
  • 30–45 minutes every other week, about six conversations
  • Kickoff, a Week 6 pulse, and a Week 12 reflection
  • Room for the conversations too personal for a group
  • Keep going past the quarter if you both want to

Alumni can come back as mentors, Circuit Leads or committee volunteers.

The path through

Join a Circuit → Present & graduate → Power Pairs → Come back as a mentor or Circuit Lead

Most of our mentors came up through the program themselves. That is the whole point.

The deal

What we give, what we ask

What you get

  • A structured twelve weeks with a start and an end
  • A mentor, or a room full of them
  • A skills self-assessment at the start and the end, so you can see the shift
  • A personal action plan, and public recognition at Graduation
  • A network that outlasts the cohort

What we ask

  • About ninety minutes a week — the Power Hour, plus your track every other week
  • Turn up, or tell your mentor you can’t
  • Mentees own the scheduling and the agenda
  • Two short feedback forms, mid-point and final
  • In a Circuit: present once, at the Showcase or at Graduation. Five minutes counts.

Getting in

How to join

  1. Watch for the interest form. Registration opens ahead of each cohort and is announced on the Women in Power LinkedIn page. One form covers mentors and mentees, and both tracks.
  2. Tell us what you are after. Networking, hard skills, soft skills — it changes where we steer you.
  3. Save the senders. Your confirmation email lists the addresses program mail comes from. Add them to your contacts or it will land in junk.
  4. Come to the first Power Hour. Everyone, one call, and you will know exactly what you signed up for.

Questions

Before you ask

Do I have to work at Microsoft?

No. Women in Power is a grassroots community organization, open to women working with and in the Power Platform ecosystem wherever they work.

I have fifteen years of experience. Is a Circuit going to be too basic?

Tracks are about your mentoring experience, not your seniority. A Circuit is less about content and more about the peer network and the habit of being seen — which senior women often need as much as anyone. If you have been through an established mentoring program elsewhere, you may be offered Power Pairs directly.

Can I be a mentor and a mentee?

Yes, and a good number of people are. Flag both on the interest form.

I’m outside the Americas. Do the times work?

We alternate group session times across zones so that everyone can make at least half the cohort live, and 1:1 pairs are matched with time zone in mind. Sessions that can be recorded, are.

Is there a fee?

No. Women in Power mentoring is run entirely by volunteers — every mentor, host and organizer does this on their own time, around a full-time job. Nobody is paid and nobody is charged. What it costs you is about ninety minutes a week.
