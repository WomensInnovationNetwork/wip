What we do

Twelve weeks, two ways in

We run mentoring in cohorts — roughly twelve weeks, kickoff to certificates — so everybody moves through it together rather than drifting off after two good conversations.

Every cohort runs two tiers side by side. Tier 1 is group mentoring, for anyone new to the programme. Tier 2 is one-to-one mentoring, for women who have been through a cohort before. They share a calendar, a kickoff and a closing session, so the two groups actually see each other rather than running in parallel silos.

What we’ve done so far

Five cohorts in

We have been running mentoring since 2024. Five cohorts have finished, every one of them staffed entirely by volunteers who did it around a full-time job.

Each cohort has changed shape based on what the last one told us. That is the part we are proudest of — not that we got it right first time, but that the feedback form actually changes the programme.

Four things five cohorts taught us

Tell people what they signed up for

The loudest complaint we ever got was that people did not know what the sessions would cover. Now every group session topic is published a week ahead, and rooms are labelled foundational or advanced.

Small rooms beat big calls

The breakout rooms were consistently the most valued part of group mentoring, and the long plenary sections the least. So the framing got shorter and the breakouts got longer.

Seniority is not the same as being new

Plenty of women arrive with twenty years of experience but no history with this programme. Tiers are now about your history with us, not your CV, and topics are levelled rather than aimed at beginners.

Everyone has to speak, once

The same handful of women were doing most of the talking. From this cohort, every 1:1 mentee presents at least once before graduating — five minutes counts, and you rehearse it with your mentor first.

The two tiers

Where you start depends on where you’ve been

Not on how senior you are. Plenty of women arrive at Tier 1 with twenty years in the industry — the tiers are about your history with this programme, not your CV.

Tier 1

Group mentoring

For anyone new to WiP mentoring.

  • Six bi-weekly one-hour sessions
  • A short framing, then breakout rooms by topic — you pick the room
  • Topics published a week ahead, and labelled foundational or advanced
  • Mentors host rooms on what they know best
  • Coffee chats with any mentor on the list, any time

Graduates who opt in move into 1:1 mentoring in the following cohort.

Tier 2

1:1 mentoring

For returning participants and Tier 1 graduates.

  • Matched with one mentor for the whole cohort
  • You meet on your own cadence — bi-weekly works for most pairs
  • Mentor meetups and mentee lightning talks through the term
  • Every mentee presents at least once before graduating
  • Matching uses experience, goals and time zone from your form

Alumni can come back as mentors, committee volunteers or community speakers.

The path through

Join Tier 1 → Graduate → Tier 2, matched 1:1 → Present & graduate → Come back as a mentor

Most of our 1:1 mentors came up through the programme themselves. That is the whole point.

Around the mentoring

The deal

What we give, what we ask

What you get

  • A structured twelve weeks with a start and an end
  • A mentor, or a room full of them
  • A skills self-assessment at the start and the end, so you can see the shift
  • A digital certificate and a LinkedIn shout-out
  • A network that outlasts the cohort

What we ask

  • About an hour a week — genuinely, that is the number
  • Turn up, or tell your mentor you can’t
  • Mentees own the scheduling and the agenda
  • Two short feedback forms, mid-point and final
  • In Tier 2: present once before you graduate. Five minutes counts.

Getting in

How to join

  1. Watch for the interest form. Registration opens ahead of each cohort and is announced on the Women in Power LinkedIn page. One form covers both mentors and mentees, and both tiers.
  2. Tell us what you are after. Networking, hard skills, soft skills — it changes where we steer you.
  3. Save the senders. Your confirmation email lists the addresses programme mail comes from. Add them to your contacts or it will land in junk.
  4. Come to kickoff. Both tiers, one call, and you will know exactly what you signed up for.

Questions

Before you ask

Do I have to work at Microsoft?

No. Women in Power is a grassroots community organisation, open to women working with and in the Power Platform ecosystem wherever they work.

I have fifteen years of experience. Is Tier 1 going to be too basic?

The tiers are about your history with this programme, not your seniority. Breakout rooms are labelled foundational or advanced so you can pick the right level, and experienced newcomers often end up hosting a room by the end.

Can I be a mentor and a mentee?

Yes, and a good number of people are. Flag both on the interest form.

I’m outside the Americas. Do the times work?

We alternate group session times across zones so that everyone can make at least half the cohort live, and 1:1 pairs are matched with time zone in mind. Sessions that can be recorded, are.

Is there a fee?

No. Women in Power mentoring is run entirely by volunteers — every mentor, host and organiser does this on their own time, around a full-time job. Nobody is paid and nobody is charged. What it costs you is the hour a week.
